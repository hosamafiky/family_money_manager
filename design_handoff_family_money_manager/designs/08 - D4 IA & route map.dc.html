REVISION 2

You asked for a Debts tab. Something has to leave.

Everything below §01 was written for five branches. Adding a sixth is not a small change — so here is the constraint, honestly, and the structure I recommend.

Why six doesn't fit

Material 3's NavigationBar takes 3–5 destinations. Six is not merely against guidance — it fails measurably here. On a 360 dp compact phone, six cells give 60 dp each, and the Arabic labels would be «الرئيسية · المعاملات · التخطيط · الالتزامات · التقارير · المزيد» (labels verbatim from app_ar.arb; see §01). «الالتزامات» is 11 characters and «المعاملات» 9; at the 10 dp label size «الالتزامات» already needs ~62 dp, and the brief requires full layout integrity at 200% text scale with no truncation.

At 200% every label truncates or the bar wraps. Truncated Arabic is not "shortened" — it is unreadable, because the letters join. So a sixth tab breaks an explicit acceptance criterion, not a style preference.

Recommended: Reports moves into More
الرئيسية الحركات ◂ التخطيط الديون المزيد

«الديون» not «الالتزامات» — six characters against eleven, fits at 200%, and it is the word a family actually uses.

Between "nine report screens two taps deep" and "six tabs that truncate", the reports lose. And the trade is smaller than it looks: reports are a browse occasionally destination, while debts have due dates — a recurring obligation earns a permanent slot far more than a chart does.

Reports keep every path, keep a hub row in More, and keep their direct deep links from the dashboard's period-flow block and spouse band — which is how they were actually reached anyway.

The alternative, if you'd rather keep Reports

Give debts a dashboard region instead of a tab. This is arguably more prominent than a tab, not less: it appears without any tap.

It also fixes something your other answer opened. You renamed the quiet strip to «إجمالي الأصول» — total assets — which is now honest but visibly half a sentence. A debts region directly beneath it completes it:

01

Verdict on the five branches

Written before Revision 2. The reasoning for keeping five branches stands; the sixth-tab request supersedes the conclusion. Read the two together — Revision 2 is what to build.

Keep them. Here's the actual argument.

The five branches map cleanly onto five different questions, which is the only test a tab bar has to pass: Home — what can I spend? Transactions — what happened? Planning — what have I committed money to? Reports — what does the pattern look like? More — set things up. No two tabs answer the same question, and no question Ahmad asks daily is missing.

The strongest case for change is that Accounts sits in More, two taps deep, despite being the second-most-visited destination in a ledger app. I considered promoting it to a tab and folding Reports into Planning. I'm not recommending it — Reports is nine screens with its own period and currency state, and burying a nine-screen branch inside a hub is worse than a two-tap Accounts. The real problem isn't which tab Accounts lives in; it's that you currently cannot open an account from Home without losing your place. That is problem 3 below, and fixing it makes the tab question moot.

Also decisive: ~1,594 tests and the shell's page-identity workaround (the sibling-absolute-route trick documented in the router's own comment) both encode this structure. Re-cutting the tabs would spend a large migration budget on a change that doesn't answer a user question better.

02

Six problems in the router as written

Ordered by how much they cost the design. The first three block screens I have already drawn.

01BLOCKS A DRAWN SCREEN

There is no route for the reversal.

/transactions/:operationId exists; nothing under it. The whole append-only correction flow — the product's most important teaching moment, drawn as D3 — has nowhere to live.

Recommendation: add /transactions/:operationId/reverse as a real route, not a bare modal. Three reasons: the sheet has required fields and a re-auth step, so it must survive process death; it needs its own back-stack entry so Android back cancels the reversal rather than leaving the detail screen; and it is the one flow where a support conversation ("send me the link") has real value.

Presented as a bottom sheet over the detail screen, so the original stays visible behind the scrim — the design depends on that adjacency.

02BLOCKS A DRAWN SCREEN

The guarded withdrawal has no home, and shouldn't get its own route.

Withdrawing from Yousuf's fund is an ordinary expense or transfer whose source happens to be a childProtectedFund. FinancialAccountType.requiresProtectedWithdrawalAudit is a property of the account, not of a separate operation.

Recommendation: no new route. /transactions/new/expense and .../transfer detect a protected source and insert the guard between form and review, so the review screen receives an already-justified operation. A separate /withdraw-protected route would let a user deep-link past the form's account validation and would duplicate the expense form.

Consequence for the design: G1's sheet is a step in an existing flow, not a screen. Its back button returns to the form with the amount intact.

03BLOCKS A DRAWN SCREEN

You cannot open an account from Home without losing Home.

/accounts/:accountId lives on branch 4. The dashboard's held region, the spouse-wallet band and every transaction detail all want to link to it. With StatefulShellRoute.indexedStack, navigating there switches the selected tab to More and discards the Home scroll position — and the bottom bar visibly jumps to a tab the user didn't choose.

Recommendation: give /accounts/:accountId, /transactions/:operationId, /budgets/:budgetId, /goals/:goalId and /certificates/:certificateId a parentNavigatorKey: rootNavigatorKey so detail screens push above the shell. The tab bar disappears (correctly — a detail page is not a destination), back returns to exactly where you were, and the current tab never changes.

This is the single highest-value router change, and it removes the argument for promoting Accounts to a tab.

04FRAGILE

state.extra carries the preselected account, so half the entry points aren't deep-linkable.

Four screens take preselectedAccountId: state.extra as String?. extra is not part of the URL, does not survive a cold start from a link, and is an unchecked cast — a wrong extra type is a runtime crash rather than a 404.

Recommendation: move it to a query parameter — /transactions/new/expense?account=<id>. Keeps every entry point linkable, keeps typed routes honest, and lets the dashboard's account rows open a pre-filled expense directly. Note that routes.dart currently only declares the two legacy typed routes, so the feature tree isn't typed at all yet — worth closing at the same time.

05FRAGILE

/transactions/new and /transactions/:operationId both match "new".

They are siblings, and only declaration order stops :operationId from swallowing new. It works today. It breaks silently the day someone reorders the list or adds a sibling above it, and the failure is "opening the create screen shows a transaction-not-found error".

Recommendation: either move creation to /transactions/create… or, better, see problem 6 — the redesign removes the need for the chooser screen entirely, and with it this ambiguity.

06THE REDESIGN DELETES A SCREEN

/transactions/new — the type chooser — is now dead weight.

It exists to ask "income, expense or transfer?". The pinned bottom action bar from the dashboard direction answers that question before navigation: three labelled buttons that go straight to the right form. Keeping the chooser costs the highest-frequency flow in the app one full tap and one full screen transition.

Recommendation: KEEP THE ROUTE, retire the destination. /transactions/new stays valid so existing links, the FAB and the widget tests that target it keep working — but nothing in the new design routes a user through it. It becomes a redirect to /transactions/new/expense once the tests are updated. Deleting the route outright would break tests for no user benefit.

Paths are verbatim from app_router.dart. KEEP = path unchanged, screen redesigned. CHANGE = routing behaviour changes. Nothing is renamed and nothing becomes unreachable.

Back-stack rule, stated once: tab switches use goBranch and never stack. Hub → list uses push inside the branch. List → detail pushes to the root navigator (problem 3), so a detail is never a tab. Detail → action (fund / release / profit / redeem / reverse) pushes above the detail. Review screens push above their form and pop back to it on edit. Saving pops the whole entry stack to the origin, not to the chooser.
