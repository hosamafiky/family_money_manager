The breakpoint moves from 840 to 905 for one reason: a 840 dp threshold catches large phones in landscape, and a rail on a phone held sideways is a rail nobody can reach. 905 is above every phone landscape width and below every tablet portrait width. Two panes never become three. A third pane would need a screen this product does not have — every detail here is terminal, and the ledger's answer to "more depth" is a drill-down, not another column.

Two widths that share a layout are one layout. The temptation at 430 dp is to split the held region into two columns; the reason not to is §3's whole argument — spendable and held live in physically separate stacked regions, and side-by-side reads as a comparison rather than a separation. So the extra 70 dp goes to margin and to surfacing the third action, which on compact lives behind «المزيد». Nothing rewraps at 200% text scale because nothing here depends on fitting three things across.

The rail is the bottom bar rotated, not a new information architecture. Same five destinations, same order, same ink-and-rule selection — an inline-end 3 px rule instead of a top one. Reordering or adding on tablet would give one household two mental models of the same app. The list keeps its full row anatomy at 300 dp — edge, direction glyph, amount, meta — because a narrower list that drops the direction edge would break the grammar §4 makes normative. Selection is an inset 2 px ink outline, not a fill: a filled row in a money list reads as a state of the money. And the detail pane has no back button, because nothing was pushed; on phone the same screen is a route, here it is a region.

The desktop board's content is deliberately the same as the tablet's. Content widths of 720 for detail and 960 for lists are already fixed in §2 and unchanged by this deliverable, so past 1280 dp the only thing that grows is the ground. The rail narrows rather than expanding into labels-beside-icons: five destinations do not need 200 dp, and a wide rail buys nothing but a longer travel to the content. No third pane, no master-detail-detail — the ledger's depth model is a drill-down that replaces the detail pane, and every detail in this product is terminal.

Platform divergence is scoped to gestures and system surfaces, and nothing else. Two behaviours differ: the iOS edge-swipe must mirror in RTL — starting from the right edge — and Android's predictive back reveals the destination behind the current screen, which only looks right once D4's defect 3 lands and detail screens push above the shell. The app-switcher cover is Android-specific in practice and is the same still-screen decision 16 · AH1 fixed for privacy mode. Everything visual stays identical. A "more Android" or "more iOS" treatment of the same ledger would produce two products, and the household that uses both would learn each twice.

The rail sits at the leading edge in both directions — right in RTL, left here — because it is chrome, not content. The list stays adjacent to it and the detail pane takes the far side, so the reading order is rail → list → detail in whichever direction the language runs.

The board AK2 was asserting without showing. The two-pane arrangement is built from order and border-inline-*, so it mirrors wholesale — and the thing worth checking is that the sequence survives: rail, then list, then detail, reading outward from the leading edge in both languages. Flipping only the panes while pinning the rail would put navigation in the middle of the screen. Masking behaves exactly as on phone — per digit group, sign and role tint kept — which is the point of testing it here: a wider layout is not a reason to show more money. The board that makes the deliverable finishable. An adaptive spec without a closed list of invariants is an invitation to redesign each screen per width, which is how a product ends up with a tablet layout that quietly drops the held-money separation because "there was room to combine them". Six invariants, four variables. Everything in the left column was argued in an earlier document — the separated regions in 01 and 03, the hatch and lock in 04 and 05, table-before-chart in 13, the consequence line in 04, the touch target and text scale in 01 — and this board's only job is to state that none of them is negotiable at any width.
