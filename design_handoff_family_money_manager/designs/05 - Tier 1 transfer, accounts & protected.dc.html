Transfer — one operation, two sides, one optional fee

The invariant made visual. A transfer is neither income nor expense and consumes no budget; only its fee is an expense. So the transfer amount is rendered hueless (transfer = achromatic #605D5D) while the fee alone takes expense red — one row tinted, everything else grey. The screen states the rule in words as well, because a user who has only ever used expense trackers will assume a transfer "costs" something.

operations already carries sourceAccountId and destinationAccountId on a single row, so the atomicity the brief demands is a property of the schema, not something the UI has to promise. The screen says "معًا أو لا شيء" because that is literally true.

Corrected — I had drawn an operation the domain forbids

This board and E4 originally sent money into a certificate through the generic transfer sheet. Having read certificate_use_cases.dart and certificate.dart: that operation does not exist. CertificatePurchaseFunding is documented as "required principal purchase during certificate creation", and CreateCertificateUseCase.execute takes principalMinorUnits and sourceAccountId together — funding happens once, inside creation. There is no top-up: the whole certificate verb list is created · purchased · profitReceived · redeemed · archived · restored · definitionRevised · purchaseReversed · profitReversed.

So this is a real spec rule, not a nicety: the transfer destination picker must exclude certificate and goalReserve accounts. Goal reserves are funded by the goal-fund flow, certificates only at creation. Offering them in a generic transfer would produce an operation the use case rejects — which is a validation error the UI should never let the user reach. The line under the destination field says so in the sheet itself.

Assumptions in these nine boards
  1. Running balance per history row (F1, F3) is the strongest honesty device on the account page and the most expensive: it needs a windowed cumulative sum in the account-ledger query, not a per-row lookup. Flag for D6.
  2. Reconciliation drift needs somewhere to store the externally-observed bank balance and its date. That is new data — a statement-balance record, not a ledger entry. The adjustment it produces is a normal ledger entry.
  3. Verified, not assumed: profit frequency and rate live on CertificateRevision (profitFrequency, annualRateBps), and the rate is explicitly "metadata only" — F2 labels it "بيانات فقط" so nobody expects the balance to accrue. Term state is derived by CertificateTermState.derive, never stored; F2's "قيد السريان" chip is that derivation, and the 232-day countdown comes from maturityDate.
  4. F2's actions now match the use cases: record profit (canRecordProfit), redeem disabled until maturity (canRedeem requires isMaturedOrOverdue — there is no early redemption in v1, which I had wrongly drawn), and cancel-purchase (ReverseCertificatePurchaseUseCase). The disabled redeem carries its reason and its date.
  5. The three suggested withdrawal reasons for a child fund are localised presets needing ARB entries.
  6. G2's "تحقّق بالبصمة ✓" implies the audit record stores which auth method passed. Worth having; may not exist.
  7. Resolved: insufficient balance rejects for cash-type accounts and warns for bank accounts (overdraft is real). Note this contradicts CurrencyAmountSummary's comment calling any negative balance a "data integrity anomaly" — that comment needs narrowing to cash accounts, or the dashboard will flag a legitimately overdrawn bank account as corrupt data.
