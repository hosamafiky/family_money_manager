Family Money Manager · Redesign · Diverge before converge

Two directions each:
dashboard and expense entry

Structurally and conceptually different, not recolours. Each is scored against Ahmad's four stated worries, then one wins and the loser is named. Artboards are 390 × 844, RTL, light theme, on the D1 tokens.

Read the repo · blocker found

Three states of money, reported through two buckets — so one whole class is invisible.

The schema has three states, not two. drift_dashboard_query_repository.dart builds spendableBalances from is_spendable = 1 AND is_protected = 0, and protectedBalances from is_protected = 1. But certificate and goal-reserve accounts are neither: app_database_certificate_schema.dart requires a certificate account to be "non-spendable, not protected" (AND fa.is_protected = 0) and locks both columns with no_modify_certificate_protected; FundPurpose.goalReserve documents reserves as "non-spendable and non-protected" with matching immutability triggers. AccountTotalsService says it outright: "Non-spendable, non-protected accounts contribute to neither total."

So the good news: certificate principal does not inflate the spendable hero — the invariant holds, and Ahmad's double-counting worry is already structurally prevented in the query. Credit where it's due.

The bad news is the opposite failure. Certificate principal and goal reserves appear in no dashboard figure at all. Ahmad's 47,900 EGP of principal and 13,600 of goal reserve are not overstated — they are unreportable. "Money I moved into a certificate might still be double-counted" is answered; "where did it go" is not. A ledger that can reconstruct any balance at any moment but whose home screen cannot name a third of the household's money is failing a different way.

And the spouse-wallet problem is real and unchanged. spouseCashWallet accounts are ordinary is_spendable = 1, is_protected = 0 accounts, so Hana's 4,382.50 is inside spendableBalances today, while SpouseWalletDashboardSummary reports it a second time. Presentation cannot subtract it — that would be authoritative balance math, forbidden by DESIGN_SYSTEM.md §8.

Proposed change (D6, query + provider work — no schema migration, the three states already exist): keep spendableBalances' definition, and add heldByReason — a per-reason breakdown over the third state, keyed certificate / goalReserve / protected — plus excludedFromAvailable for spouse wallets so the hero can name what it left out. Both directions below are drawn against that shape. Answering open question #2: the spouse wallet must be excluded, and today it isn't.

Decision

1a The Sheet wins.

Why B lost: its central idea — money as adjacent locations — is precisely what causes Ahmad's double-counting worry. Putting a held vault in the same horizontal row as three spendable vaults invites mental addition, and no amount of hatching undoes a row. It also hides the spouse wallet, which is a daily verbal conversation, behind a tap. B is the better-looking screen and the worse ledger.

What I'm taking from B: the inverted ink hero (it earns the dominance the brief demands far better than white-on-white), the pinned bottom action bar with expense as the wide primary, and the explicit "يحتاج انتباهك (2)" counted heading — a labelled count beats an unlabelled stack of warnings. 1a as built for Tier 1 will carry all three.

Decision

1c Numpad sheet wins.

Why D lost: it is the more charming idea and it fails the two hardest constraints in the brief at once — 200% text scale and long wrapping Arabic account names. A sentence is a fixed grammar; a ledger entry has eight optional fields and unbounded strings. D also can't hold the optional field set without becoming a form again, at which point it is C with worse ergonomics.

What I'm taking from D — two things, and they matter. (1) The recurring-sentence tiles become "repeat" shortcuts pinned above the pad, which is genuinely 2 taps and beats C's 3 for Ahmad's daily groceries. (2) The sentence itself becomes the confirmation and review copy: after saving, the snackbar and the review screen both read «صرفت 382.50 ج.م على بقالة من محفظتي» — a natural-language read-back is a far better error check than a table of labelled rows, and it costs nothing structurally.

Also confirmed in the repo: expense_form_screen.dart is 22.5 KB and record_expense_use_case.dart already handles idempotency, so C is a presentation-only change — the field set, validation and the review hand-off all survive. expense_review_screen.dart becomes optional rather than mandatory, reached by the "مراجعة" secondary button.
