01

Findings and stance

Kept — it earned its place
  • All 16 AppFinancialColors role names. The code depends on them. Values are replaced wholesale; not one key is renamed.
  • All 11 semantic type roles. Naming type by financial job rather than by size is genuinely good and rare.
  • The 4/8/12/16/20/24/32/40 spacing scale. Modernist's own scale is 4/8/12/16/24/32; the existing scale is a superset, so it survives and Modernist's variables map onto it.
  • Presentation-only components. No component learns balance math or currency logic. Reinforced, not relaxed.
  • Five-branch shell, pending the D4 audit.
Replaced
  • Seed green 0xFF1A6B3C and every financial hue. The green/red income–expense axis is the app's single largest accessibility failure; it cannot be fixed by choosing better greens.
  • Rounded M3 cards + elevation. Radius goes to 0. Elevation becomes rules and tonal fill.
  • Equal-weight card stacks. Replaced by a ruled two-region hierarchy.
  • Roboto / M3 defaults. Archivo + IBM Plex Sans Arabic.
  • The separate reports widget kit. Folded into the same primitives (D2).
Thesis — three sentences

Money is filed, not decorated. Every amount sits in a ruled, labelled position that states its location, owner and spendability before its colour says anything at all.

Spendable money and held money live in physically separate regions, so "can I spend this" is answered by layout and survives greyscale, blur and a screen reader.

Red means one thing only — money leaving — which is why the primary action button is ink, not accent.

Two things the brief asked for that I have to flag before designing further

1 · Modernist's primary-action rule is overridden. Modernist says the primary action is a solid accent (red) fill. In a ledger, red must be unambiguous and mean only outflow. Primary actions therefore take a solid ink fill (primaryAction = #201E1D); accent red is spent only on the expense role, destructive confirmation, and the single red poster statement Modernist reserves for a section break. Declared departure #1.

2 · The repo could not be read this turn. GitHub is not connected, so docs/* and the component sources were not opened; this spec is built from the brief's own inventory, which lists every token key, type role, spacing step and component. D6's file-by-file mapping and the migration phases require repo access — connect GitHub and I will produce them against the real tree rather than guessing at paths.

02

The four hardest calls

Each is decided here, in the language spec, because each one is a token-and-component decision rather than a screen decision. Screens inherit them.

CALL 1

Spendable vs. held money

Decision: separate regions, four independent signals. Held money never appears in the same list as spendable money — not greyed out in it, not sorted to the bottom of it. It lives below a 2 px rule, on the recessed surface, under its own heading, and it is never summed with anything above that rule.

LayoutSeparate ruled region + its own heading. The primary signal; needs no vision at all. ShapeA 4 px leading edge: solid for spendable, 45° hatched for held. Survives full greyscale. TextEvery held row is labelled — غير قابل للصرف · "not spendable" — plus its reason (certificate / goal / protected). SemanticsScreen-reader label leads with the class: "held, not spendable, certificate principal, 47,900 pounds".

Why it survives privacy mode: masking removes the digits, not the region, the rule, the hatch or the label. A blurred dashboard still tells you which money is yours to spend.

CALL 2

Expense entry in three taps

Decision: numpad-first sheet, no modes. The sheet opens with the keypad already up and the amount field focused. Category is a horizontal chip row directly above the pad, ordered by recency for the chosen account. Save is pinned and live from the first digit.

Tap 1 — amount, typed on the already-open pad
Tap 2 — a category chip
Tap 3 — حفظ Save

Everything defaulted: account = last used, date = today, spender = the user, scope = household, beneficiary = none. Each default is printed in a single meta line under the amount, so nothing is hidden — it is pre-answered and visible.

The full case needs no mode switch. The pad collapses when the sheet is dragged up, revealing spender, beneficiary, scope, notes, receipt and recurrence as ordinary ruled form rows. One gesture, same sheet, same save button — a power user is never sent to a different screen, and a fast user never sees the fields.

CALL 3

Reports stop reading as a separate app

Decision: delete the report widget kit. There is no such thing as a report component. A report is a screen built from the same nine primitives the rest of the app uses — SectionHeader, CurrencyAmountRow, FinancialMetric, ProgressMeter, TransactionListTile, PeriodSelector, FilterChipGroup, StatusBadge, AppEmptyState.

Charts get exactly three new primitives (BarSeries, LineSeries, ShareBar) which consume the same tokens, the same 4 px rhythm, the same 2 px rules, and the same direction encoding as a transaction row. A bar in a report and an amount in a list are tinted from the identical variable.

And every chart is preceded, not followed, by the ruled figure table it draws — which is simultaneously the accessibility alternative and the reason reports feel like the ledger they came from.

CALL 4

Multi-currency without a total

Decision: a stacked currency ledger, one dominant row. The primary currency (EGP) is rendered at displayBalance. Every other currency present follows beneath it at reportValue, one per hairline-separated row, each with its ISO code. No switcher — hiding a currency behind a control is how money gets forgotten.

The absence of a total is stated, not implied. Where a grand total would sit, the block prints a rule and the line لا يوجد إجمالي موحّد — كل عملة مستقلة ("no combined total — each currency is independent"). Users read a missing total as a bug; a labelled refusal teaches the invariant instead.

Non-money holdings (50 g of 21k gold) are a quantity row, never a currency row: 50.000 g · 21k, with its EGP valuation shown as a separately-labelled, explicitly derived figure — تقدير "estimate" — because a gold valuation is a market price, not a ledger fact.

Why this survives deuteranopia and protanopia

The old palette put income and expense on a green–red axis, which is precisely the axis both common dichromacies collapse. Two structural fixes, not a hue tweak: (a) the direction axis moves to teal vs. red-orange — separated on the blue–yellow channel that both dichromacies retain — and (b) the three class roles (protected / goal / certificate), which are all blues and bronzes and would be confusable with each other, never appear in the same context as one another, because each one owns its own labelled region. Direction roles mark amounts; class roles mark regions. They never compete.

05 · D1

Typography

Why IBM Plex Sans Arabic

It is a naskh-grotesque: low-contrast, open apertures, short and even ascenders, and — decisively — lining tabular numerals of real quality in both Western and Arabic-Indic sets. Family Money Manager is a column of numbers; a family whose Arabic looks handsome but whose digits don't align is a worse product.

It also sits next to Archivo without a seam — both are neo-grotesques on a similar x-height, so a mixed Arabic-with-Latin-numerals run (the app's most common string) does not visibly change font mid-line.

Rejected: Noto Kufi Arabic — architecturally on-brief, but Kufi's flattened terminals cost legibility at 11–13 px, which is where supportingMeta lives. Cairo — very familiar in Egypt, but its numerals are narrower than its letters and it has no true tabular set. Both are switchable in this document's Tweaks to compare.

Numerals — the ar_EG call

Western digits (0–9) are the default, in both locales. Egyptian banking, ATM slips, receipts and mobile-wallet SMS all print Western digits; Arabic-Indic (٠١٢٣) reads as formal or archaic in a money context and, more practically, mixes badly with the Latin currency codes (USD, EGP) and the Latin-digit dates users see everywhere else.

Arabic-Indic is offered as a Settings preference. financialAmount, displayBalance and reportValue always render tabular lining figures (font-feature-settings:"tnum","lnum") in whichever set, so a column of amounts aligns on the decimal at every size.

Three rules make this work. (1) No money row is ever a fixed-height row — every tile is intrinsic-height with 12 px vertical padding. (2) Amount and currency code are a flex-wrap pair, so the code drops below the number rather than truncating it. (3) Labels and amounts are never on the same baseline in a constrained width — the tile switches from row to column below 148 dp of available label space.

Mixed-script runs — the app's most common string
تم تحويل 47,900.00 ج.م من حساب بنك مصر إلى شهادة استثمار 12 شهرًا بتاريخ 2026/07/12 — رسوم 15.00 ج.م.

Every numeric run is wrapped in a first-strong isolate so the bidi algorithm cannot reorder 2026/07/12 or move a minus sign. Dates use yyyy/MM/dd in ar_EG (as printed on Egyptian bank statements) and d MMM yyyy in en.

Don't: centre Arabic body copy, letter-space Arabic, synthesise a bold from a 400 weight, or let an amount inherit the paragraph's direction. Each one is a bug we will see in review.

07 · D2

Component library

Artboards are RTL and light unless labelled. Measurements are in dp. Every component below is presentation-only: it receives formatted strings, a role enum and a privacy flag — it never queries, never sums, never classifies.

The privacy contract, stated once and inherited by every money component: a masked amount renders a solid ink bar per digit group at 0.62 em height, keeps its currency code, keeps its sign and glyph, keeps its exact layout width, and exposes semanticsLabel: "hidden amount". Bars, not blur: blur is expensive on long lists, survives screenshots imperfectly, and still leaks magnitude. PrivacyScope.of(context).masked is read by FinancialAmountText alone — no other component implements masking.

One rule, both features. Consumption is always ink — for budgets and goals, because ink is "how much of the plan is used up" and carries no judgement. The remainder is a hatch. Overshoot past 100% adds a second hatch band outside the mark, and that band carries the role: expense for an over-budget, goalReserved for an overfunded goal. So the state is readable as geometry, in greyscale, and as text; the percentage is never the only signal; and the same widget serves both screens.

Do not fill a goal meter in goalReserved. An earlier goal-detail artboard did exactly that, which quietly made ProgressMeter two components again — the precise failure (audit problem 7) this library exists to prevent. Fill = ink. Hatch = role.

Why these two are heavier, and how much heavier
FrameA 2 px protectedMoney border with a 6 px top edge. Nothing else in the app has a coloured frame — it is the whole vocabulary of "this is different". Tinted headerThe only tinted sheet header in the product. Arithmetic shownBefore / amount / after, with the child's remaining balance as the emphasised figure. The user sees the consequence, not a total. Two required fieldsReason and beneficiary. Both block the confirm; both are printed on the audit entry. Explicit acknowledgementA checkbox whose label names the child. Deliberately a sentence, not "I agree". Re-authBiometric or PIN after confirm, before the write. Heavy hapticOne of only two in the app.

Not alarming, not shaming. The copy never says "are you sure" and never implies wrongdoing — spending a child's gift money on that child's school fees is the normal case. It says what will be recorded and to whom. Weight comes from frame, arithmetic and required reasoning; not from red, not from an exclamation mark, and not from a scolding tone.

Open questions — assumptions I made
  1. Western digits as the ar_EG default. Reversible, but it changes every specimen above.
  2. Spouse wallet is excluded from "available to spend." The brief says "contextually". I assumed excluded, with given/spent/returned/remaining shown separately — Ahmad's worry is telling his money from Hana's, and including her wallet in his spendable figure defeats that.
  3. Gold is a quantity row, and its EGP valuation is labelled an estimate. This implies a price source the ledger doesn't own.
  4. reversal_reason and reversed_by_entry_id are new fields. Presentation cannot fake either.
  5. Privacy masking is implemented in FinancialAmountText only, via an inherited scope — no widget masks itself.
  6. The five-branch shell is provisionally kept; I audit it properly in D4 and may move Reports.
  7. Egyptian date format yyyy/MM/dd in ar, d MMM yyyy in en.
  8. Certificate interest is income-coloured while principal is certificatePrincipal — two roles inside one card, deliberately.
